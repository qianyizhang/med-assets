# Diaphragm — static source study, not a finished animated asset

**Improvement:** the previous shallow schematic cap is compared with a separate Z-Anatomy atlas model showing a deeper asymmetric dome, inferior shell and existing openings/projections. Same-source lower ribs, sternum and lumbar context make regional placement easier to inspect.

**Scope:** gross static shape and regional context only. No detailed-anatomy, breathing-motion, wholebody-integration or clinical-validation claim. Comparison is between different models, with different framing; not registered anatomy.

**Limits:** smooth surfaces; no differentiated central tendon or muscle fibres; narrow descending source projections remain unexplained; anatomical opening names are not assigned. Four source boundary edges remain. Context excludes cartilage and much of the thorax; free rib ends do not establish attachments. No browser animation/runtime validation.

**What was checked:** 9 rendered directions, source-coordinate/face fidelity, unchanged native transforms, deterministic GLB export and geometry roundtrip. Technical checks establish preserved geometry—not anatomical quality. Existing animation was not transferred.

**Lesson:** counting organs and passing mesh/export tests can hide weak visual anatomy. Review isolated silhouettes, undersides and coherent context before expanding coverage; keep accepted scope narrow and unresolved source details visible rather than inventing repairs.

**Reference:** David Morrice, “Thoracic anatomy”, *Core Topics in Thoracic Anesthesia* (Cambridge University Press, 2009), printed pp.7–8/Fig.1.4. Peripheral muscle/central tendon and rib/lumbar relationships are reference targets, not verified labels on this model.
