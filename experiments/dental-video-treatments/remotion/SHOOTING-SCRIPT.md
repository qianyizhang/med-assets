# The Decay File

A 48-second narrated dental explainer. This Remotion version interprets the same
voiceover as the HyperFrames and ManimGL versions using warm editorial dioramas,
an investigative opening and an assessment decision map. The imagery is original,
dimensional vector illustration, not validated 3D anatomy or an operative guide.

## 01 / The quiet start · 0–6 s

**Narration:** Tooth decay starts before a hole. What tips the balance?

**Picture:** Large typographic hook beside a gently turning tooth on a small
specimen plinth. An inspection ring draws attention to a tiny region; a mirror
suggests looking more closely. The film begins with a question rather than a
list of definitions.

## 02 / The pressure · 6–14 s

**Narration:** Some bacteria in plaque use sugars to make acids. Repeated exposure
can keep enamel losing minerals.

**Picture:** Sugar cubes, plaque bacteria and acid drops form a left-to-right
causal chain. The tooth becomes a small cutaway at the end of the chain. Mineral
particles drift away; repeated exposure markers accumulate beneath the objects.
No numerical biological rates or material quantities are implied.

## 03 / The response · 14–23 s

**Narration:** Saliva and fluoride help replace lost minerals. Early decay can
sometimes be stopped or reversed before a cavity forms.

**Picture:** A saliva droplet and fluoride toothpaste prop feed a returning stream
of mineral symbols toward the tooth. A balanced-arrow locator replaces a success
checkmark. The qualifier “Can sometimes be reversed” remains prominent.

## 04 / The threshold · 23–31 s

**Narration:** If loss keeps winning, the surface can break down. A formed cavity
needs dental assessment and appropriate treatment.

**Picture:** Enlarge the tooth and reveal a bounded brown cavity shape. A locator
connects the surface change to an orange threshold card. A mirror enters, followed
by “Next: dental assessment.” This is a schematic state change, not a preparation
shape or prediction of an individual lesion.

## 05 / The decision · 31–40 s

**Narration:** A dentist assesses the tooth and selects treatment. A filling is
one common way to restore damaged structure.

**Picture:** A stepped decision map moves from assessment to an individualized
plan. One branch reveals a simplified restoration state; a second preserves the
fact that other options depend on assessment. No bur, operative angle, depth,
material claim, tool sequence or procedural instruction is depicted.

## 06 / The long game · 40–48 s

**Narration:** The long game is prevention: fluoride toothpaste twice daily,
cleaning between teeth, and regular dental review.

**Picture:** Three prop-based dioramas assemble: toothbrush/toothpaste,
interdental cleaner, and mirror/review calendar. The final message is a practical
routine rather than a guaranteed treatment outcome.

The shared voiceover's high-level claims were checked against
[NIDCR tooth-decay education](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/tooth-decay),
[NIDCR's decay process explanation](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/tooth-decay/more-info/tooth-decay-process),
and [FDA treatment options](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/dental-amalgam-fillings/treatment-options-dental-caries).
These sources do not validate the drawn tooth, cavity shape or treatment selection.
