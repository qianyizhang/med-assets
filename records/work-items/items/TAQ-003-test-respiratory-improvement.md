## Breathing view trial · 2026-11-22

**Decision.** Keep the anterior phase comparison for bilateral orientation and retain the new side view as a supplementary local teaching plate. The editable assembly and its existing presentation retain their current identities. The maintainer can adopt the supplement if the lesson should explicitly teach flattening of the dome, in addition to the visible descent of the lower lung edges.

The baseline problem is liver occlusion in the frontal view. The desired outcome is to follow the tan dome crest and outer rim from expiration to peak inspiration while retaining one visible lung base and the liver for orientation. A candidate fails as a supplement if the crest remains obscured at both phases, or the lung base or liver lose their recognizable silhouettes. A side view cannot replace the anterior phase pair when comparison of both lungs is the task.

[Whole comparison](../../../reviews/teaching-assessments/2026-11-22-respiratory/motion-whole.jpg) · [Matched detail](../../../reviews/teaching-assessments/2026-11-22-respiratory/motion-detail.jpg) · [Exact assembly and camera receipt](../../../reviews/teaching-assessments/2026-11-22-respiratory/motion-compare.json) · [Evaluated motion bounds](../../../reviews/teaching-assessments/2026-11-22-respiratory/motion.json)

In each comparison the anterior baseline is the upper row and the side candidate is the lower row. Expiration is on the left and peak inspiration on the right. Both camera pairs use the same orthographic crop within each plate, identical lighting, the exact assembly and the same seven visible meshes. Look beneath the lower lung edge and beside the reddish liver. The side view exposes more of the tan dome at expiration and its flatter silhouette at peak inspiration. The front view leaves the middle of the crest behind the liver. In the side view the near and far lungs overlap, so the learner loses the direct bilateral comparison. The detail makes the relative motion more visible while cropping out the apices. These views illustrate authored motion and do not establish diaphragm attachments, contact mechanics or motion validity.
